EV Charger Installation Guide for Austin Homeowners: Level 1, 2, and What You Actually Need

By Texas Electric and Light — Licensed Electricians, TECL #28315

Austin has one of the highest electric vehicle adoption rates in Texas, and more homeowners are asking us about home EV charging every month. The good news: adding home charging is straightforward for most Austin homes. The better news: Austin Energy offers rebates that reduce your out-of-pocket cost. Here is everything you need to know.

Level 1 vs. Level 2 Charging: What's the Difference?

Your electric vehicle can charge at different speeds depending on the equipment you use:

Level 1 Charging

Level 1 charging uses a standard 120-volt household outlet — the same kind your phone charger uses. Most EVs come with a Level 1 cord. The limitation: Level 1 adds roughly 3-5 miles of range per hour of charging. If you drive 30-40 miles per day and have 8-10 hours overnight to charge, Level 1 may be sufficient. If you drive more, you will regularly wake up to an insufficiently charged vehicle.

No installation required for Level 1 — just plug in. But you should verify the outlet is on a dedicated circuit and is in good condition. A shared circuit that is also powering other garage equipment is a potential overload.

Level 2 Charging

Level 2 uses a 240-volt circuit — the same voltage as your dryer or oven. A Level 2 charger adds 20-30 miles of range per hour, which means most drivers fully charge overnight regardless of how much they drove that day. Level 2 requires installation by a licensed electrician.

For most Austin EV drivers, Level 2 is the right choice. The installation cost is modest relative to the convenience, and the Austin Energy rebate reduces it further.

What Does Level 2 Charger Installation Involve?

A standard Level 2 EV charger installation involves:

  1. Panel evaluation — your electrician confirms your panel has capacity for the new circuit (typically 50-60 amps for Level 2)
  2. New dedicated circuit — a dedicated 240-volt circuit run from your panel to the charging location
  3. Outlet or hardwired connection — either a NEMA 14-50 outlet (which accepts the charger's plug) or a hardwired connection depending on the charger
  4. Charger mounting — the charging unit mounted in your garage or at the designated outdoor location
  5. Permit and inspection — required in Austin; your electrician pulls the permit and coordinates the inspection

A standard installation typically takes 4-6 hours. If your panel needs upgrading to accommodate the new circuit, that adds time and cost.

Which Charger Should You Buy?

For Tesla owners, the Tesla Wall Connector is the optimal choice — it communicates directly with the vehicle and supports load sharing if you later add a second charger. For non-Tesla EVs, look for a UL-listed Level 2 charger from established brands (ChargePoint, JuiceBox, Enel X). We are brand-agnostic — we install what works best for your vehicle and situation.

Key specs to look for: at least 40 amps output (50 amp circuit), Wi-Fi connectivity for scheduling and monitoring, and weatherproof rating if mounting outdoors.

Austin Energy EV Charger Rebate

Austin Energy offers rebates for qualifying Level 2 EV charger installations. The program details and rebate amounts change periodically, so verify current availability at austinenergy.com or ask your electrician — we stay current on rebate programs and can help you qualify.

To qualify for most Austin Energy rebates, the charger must be installed by a licensed electrician with a permit, and the charger must be enrolled in Austin Energy's demand response program (which allows Austin Energy to briefly reduce charging speed during peak grid demand).

Austin-Specific Tip: Austin Energy's time-of-use rates reward EV owners who charge overnight (typically midnight to 6 AM) with significantly lower electricity costs. A smart Level 2 charger with scheduling lets you automatically charge during off-peak hours, reducing your monthly charging costs substantially.

Can Your Panel Handle It?

This is the most common question we get. A Level 2 charger on a 50-amp circuit adds meaningful load to your panel. For homes with a 200-amp panel, this is rarely a problem. For homes with older 100-amp panels — common in Austin neighborhoods built before 1990 — it may push the panel to capacity, especially if you also have electric dryers, electric range, or a hot tub.

We assess panel capacity as part of every EV charger installation consultation. If an upgrade is needed, we can often combine the panel upgrade and charger installation into one project.

Key Takeaways

  • Level 2 charging adds 20-30 miles per hour — right for most Austin EV drivers
  • Installation requires a licensed electrician, a dedicated 240V circuit, and a permit
  • Austin Energy offers EV charger rebates — ask your electrician about current availability
  • Charge overnight during off-peak hours to minimize electricity costs on Austin Energy's TOU rate
  • Have your panel evaluated before installation — older 100-amp panels may need upgrading

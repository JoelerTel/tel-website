Home Automation Wiring Basics: What to Run While Your Walls Are Open

By Texas Electric and Light — Licensed Electricians, TECL #28315

The best time to wire a home for automation is during construction or major renovation — when walls are open, running cable costs a fraction of what it costs to retrofit through finished walls later. The challenge is knowing what to run before you have decided exactly what smart home system you want.

Our approach: run infrastructure that gives you maximum flexibility for whatever system you choose. Here is what we recommend for Austin homeowners during any significant renovation.

CAT6 Ethernet — Run It Everywhere You Might Want It

Wi-Fi is convenient but not always reliable — especially for devices that need to be always-on and responsive. Wired ethernet connections are faster, more reliable, and do not compete with the dozens of other Wi-Fi devices in a modern home.

Run CAT6 to:

  • Every television location
  • Home office and desk locations
  • Media room and home theater
  • Security camera locations (exterior corners, driveway, front door)
  • Each floor's access point location (for distributed Wi-Fi)
  • The location of your router and networking equipment
  • Doorbell and intercom locations

CAT6 cable is inexpensive — running an extra drop while walls are open costs very little extra. Skipping it and deciding you want it later means cutting drywall, fishing through insulation, and patching. Run more than you think you need.

Speaker Wire for In-Wall and In-Ceiling Audio

If there is any possibility you want whole-home or zone audio — music in the kitchen, covered patio, master bedroom — run speaker wire to those locations during renovation. Speaker wire through finished walls is one of the most difficult retrofits we do.

Common locations: great room, kitchen, master bedroom, covered patio, outdoor entertainment area, home theater. Run to ceiling locations where in-ceiling speakers would go, and to the wall where a volume control might be mounted.

Conduit for Future Flexibility

In locations where you are not sure what you will want — but want the flexibility — run empty conduit. A 3/4-inch conduit from your structured wiring panel location to the attic, for example, lets you pull whatever cable you decide you need in the future without opening walls again.

Similarly, conduit from your electrical panel location to the exterior — for a future EV charger, generator connection, or solar panels — is far less expensive to install during construction than after.

Structured Wiring Panel Location

All the low-voltage wiring — ethernet, coax, speaker wire, security, doorbell — should terminate in a central location: a structured wiring panel or media closet. This is typically a small closet or dedicated cabinet where your networking equipment, cable modem, router, and AV distribution equipment live.

Plan this location early — it needs power outlets (a dedicated circuit is ideal), should be accessible, and ideally has some climate consideration since networking equipment generates heat. In Austin's climate, a closet that gets very hot is not ideal for sensitive electronics.

Smart Lighting Infrastructure

If you plan to use a smart lighting system, a few things are worth planning during rough-in:

  • Neutral wires at switch boxes — many smart switches require a neutral wire that older homes often lack at the switch location. Running proper 3-wire cable during renovation eliminates this problem.
  • Dedicated switch legs — plan switch locations before drywall so you do not have to add conduit runs later
  • Under-cabinet outlet or junction box — for hardwired LED under-cabinet lighting

Key Takeaways

  • Run CAT6 ethernet to every TV, desk, camera, and access point location — it is cheap during renovation and expensive after
  • Speaker wire to audio locations is extremely difficult to add after walls are closed
  • Plan a central structured wiring panel location with power and access
  • Run neutral wires to switch boxes during renovation to support any smart switch system
  • Empty conduit to strategic locations gives maximum future flexibility

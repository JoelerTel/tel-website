Storm Electrical Preparation: Protecting Your Austin Home Before the Weather Hits

By Texas Electric and Light — Licensed Electricians, TECL #28315

Austin and Central Texas get hit by severe weather more than most people expect — violent Hill Country thunderstorms, hail events that can knock out power, and of course the lesson everyone in Texas learned in February 2021 when the winter storm took down the grid for days. Electrical preparedness is not optional in Central Texas. It is something every homeowner should address before the season, not during it.

Here is what we recommend from 20+ years of serving Austin homes through every kind of storm the region throws at us.

Surge Protection: Your First Line of Defense

When power goes out and then comes back — especially after a storm or grid fluctuation — the returning power often arrives as a surge. Voltage spikes can destroy electronics, appliances, and HVAC systems in milliseconds. This is one of the most common and most preventable forms of storm-related electrical damage.

There are two levels of surge protection worth understanding:

  • Point-of-use surge protectors — power strips with surge protection for individual devices. These protect what is plugged into them and are an inexpensive first step.
  • Whole-home surge protection — a device installed at your electrical panel that protects your entire home's electrical system. This is the right solution for Austin homeowners who want comprehensive protection. A licensed electrician can install one in a few hours.

Whole-home surge protection is especially important for homes with smart home systems, high-end appliances, EV chargers, and sensitive electronics.

Standby Generators: The February 2021 Lesson

The 2021 winter storm changed how a lot of Central Texas homeowners think about backup power. Being without electricity for days — in freezing temperatures, with no heat, no water (when pipes froze), and no way to charge devices — is a genuine safety risk, not just an inconvenience.

A whole-home standby generator, connected to your natural gas or propane supply, turns on automatically when power fails and keeps your home running normally until the grid recovers. Generac is the most common brand we install, sized to the specific loads of each home.

The installation requires a licensed electrician to install the transfer switch (which safely isolates your home from the grid while the generator runs), connect the generator, and coordinate the gas connection. Permits are required. Plan ahead — generator installations typically take 2-4 weeks from order to completion, and demand spikes dramatically after every major storm event.

Austin-Specific Note: Natural gas remained available during the 2021 winter storm when the electric grid failed. Homes with natural gas standby generators fared significantly better than those without backup power.

What To Do Before a Storm Hits

  • Locate and label your main breaker — everyone in your household should know where it is and how to turn it off
  • Charge all devices and backup batteries before the storm arrives
  • Unplug sensitive electronics — televisions, computers, and audio equipment — before a major storm. A surge protector helps, but nothing beats disconnecting entirely
  • Know your utility's outage reporting number — Austin Energy: 512-322-9100
  • Do not use generators indoors — CO poisoning kills people every storm season. Generators must run outside, well away from windows and doors

After the Storm: Before You Turn Everything Back On

If your home experienced flooding, standing water near your electrical panel, or physical damage, do not turn your power back on until a licensed electrician has inspected the affected electrical systems. Water and electricity interact in dangerous ways that are not always visible — wet insulation, corroded connections, and damaged devices can cause fires or shocks after the water has receded.

If you smell burning after power is restored, turn off your main breaker and call an electrician immediately.

Long-Term Electrical Storm Preparedness

Beyond immediate preparation, these upgrades make your home more resilient to storm-related electrical damage over the long term:

  • Whole-home surge protection at the panel
  • Standby generator with automatic transfer switch
  • Battery backup systems (Tesla Powerwall, etc.) for shorter outages and solar integration
  • Updated panel with properly rated breakers for all circuits

Key Takeaways

  • Install whole-home surge protection at your panel before storm season — not after
  • A standby generator requires advance planning — order before storm season, not during it
  • Unplug sensitive electronics before a major storm arrives
  • Never run a generator indoors — CO poisoning is deadly
  • After any flooding, have a licensed electrician inspect before restoring power to affected areas

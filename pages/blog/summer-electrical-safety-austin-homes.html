10 Essential Summer Electrical Safety Tips for Austin Homeowners

By Texas Electric and Light — Licensed Electricians, TECL #28315

Austin summers are brutal. When temperatures climb past 100 degrees for weeks at a stretch, your home's electrical system takes a beating — and most homeowners don't realize it until something goes wrong. Air conditioners running around the clock, fans in every room, refrigerators working overtime, and everyone home cranking up screens and devices. The demand on your electrical system during a Central Texas summer is unlike any other time of year.

As licensed electricians who have served Austin since 2004, we respond to more emergency calls in June, July, and August than any other months. Most of those calls could have been prevented. Here are the ten electrical safety steps we recommend every Austin homeowner take before the heat peaks.

1. Know Your Panel's Capacity

Most homes built before 1990 have 100-amp or 150-amp electrical service. That was fine when homes had fewer devices, smaller AC units, and no EV chargers. Today, a typical Austin household running two AC systems, a refrigerator, washer/dryer, home office equipment, and several televisions can easily push a 100-amp panel to its limit during a heat wave.

Signs your panel is struggling: breakers trip frequently, lights flicker when the AC kicks on, or you notice a burning smell near the panel. If you are seeing any of these, call a licensed electrician before summer hits full stride — not during it, when every electrician in Austin is already booked.

Austin-Specific Note: Austin Energy reports peak demand days typically occur in late July and August when temperatures exceed 105°F. Having your panel evaluated before peak season can prevent the most dangerous failures.

2. Test Your GFCI Outlets Monthly

Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter outlets — the ones with the test and reset buttons — are required in kitchens, bathrooms, garages, and outdoor areas. They are designed to cut power instantly if they detect a dangerous current leak, which can happen when humidity is high and electrical systems are under stress.

Testing takes 30 seconds: press the "test" button — the outlet should lose power. Press "reset" — power should return. If it doesn't respond correctly, the GFCI has failed and needs replacement. A failed GFCI provides no protection at all.

3. Do Not Overload Outdoor Outlets and Extension Cords

Summer in Austin means outdoor entertaining — string lights, outdoor speakers, fans, grills with side burners, and portable coolers. All of that adds up fast. Extension cords are rated for specific loads, and exceeding them causes overheating that can melt insulation and start fires.

The fix: have a licensed electrician add dedicated outdoor circuits for your patio, pool area, or outdoor kitchen. A properly installed weatherproof outlet costs far less than the alternative.

4. Inspect Your Air Conditioner's Electrical Connections

Your AC unit's outdoor condenser has electrical connections that expand and contract with Austin's extreme temperature swings. Over time, these connections can loosen, causing arcing — a major fire hazard. Your HVAC company handles the mechanical side, but the electrical disconnect, wiring, and breaker serving the unit should be inspected by a licensed electrician every few years.

Also ensure the breaker for your AC unit matches the nameplate rating on the equipment. Mismatched breakers are one of the most common code violations we find.

5. Keep Your Electrical Panel Clear and Accessible

It sounds simple, but we regularly show up to homes where the electrical panel is blocked by furniture, storage, or — in older Austin homes — behind a framed picture or in a closet packed with boxes. Code requires 36 inches of clear space in front of the panel. During a summer emergency, you need to reach that panel fast.

6. Watch for Flickering Lights

Occasional flickering when a large appliance starts is normal. Persistent flickering — especially in multiple rooms — is not. It usually indicates a loose connection somewhere in your wiring, a failing breaker, or a problem with your utility connection. Loose connections arc and cause heat. In Austin's hot attics, that heat has less room for error.

Do not ignore flickering lights. Call an electrician.

7. Be Smart About Space Heaters — And Portable AC Units

Many Austin homeowners run portable AC units in rooms the central system can't keep cool enough. These units draw significant power — often 15 amps on a 15-amp circuit — leaving no margin for anything else. Plug portable units directly into a wall outlet, never an extension cord, and make sure the circuit is dedicated or lightly loaded.

8. Check Smoke and Carbon Monoxide Detectors

Heat waves stress HVAC systems, and struggling systems sometimes produce CO. Test your detectors monthly and replace batteries at least annually. Detectors older than 10 years should be replaced entirely — the sensors degrade over time regardless of battery life.

9. Be Cautious With Pools and Outdoor Water Features

Austin's pool culture means a lot of electrical systems operating in wet environments all summer. Pool pumps, lighting, and automation systems should be inspected annually by a licensed electrician. The most dangerous situation — electric shock drowning — occurs when current leaks into pool water, and it is almost always preventable with proper bonding and GFCI protection.

Warning: If you or a swimmer feel a tingling sensation in pool water, exit immediately and do not re-enter until a licensed electrician has inspected the pool's electrical system.

10. Know Your Emergency Contacts

Save your electrician's number before you need it. Austin Energy's outage line: 512-322-9100. Our emergency line: (512) 547-4130. In a genuine electrical emergency — burning smell, sparks, a panel that won't reset — turn off the main breaker and call a licensed electrician immediately.

Key Takeaways

  • Have your panel evaluated before peak summer season if it's older than 20 years
  • Test GFCI outlets monthly — a failed GFCI provides zero protection
  • Never plug portable AC units into extension cords
  • Flickering lights and frequent tripped breakers are warning signs, not inconveniences
  • Pool electrical should be inspected annually by a licensed electrician
  • Know how to reach your electrician and Austin Energy before you need them

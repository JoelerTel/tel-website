Home Electrical Safety Inspection Checklist for Austin Homeowners

By Texas Electric and Light — Licensed Electricians, TECL #28315

You do not need to be an electrician to identify many common electrical hazards in your home. A systematic walkthrough — using this checklist — takes about 30 minutes and can reveal issues that deserve professional attention before they become emergencies.

This checklist is designed for homeowners who want to understand their home's electrical condition. It is not a substitute for a licensed electrical inspection, but it will help you identify concerns and have an informed conversation with an electrician.

Important: Do not open your electrical panel yourself unless you are qualified. The inside of an electrical panel contains live conductors that remain energized even when breakers are turned off. Leave panel inspection to licensed electricians.

Section 1: Electrical Panel (Exterior Only)

  • ☐ Panel is accessible — 36 inches of clear space in front, not blocked by storage or furniture
  • ☐ Panel door closes and latches properly
  • ☐ No rust, moisture, or corrosion visible on the panel exterior
  • ☐ Panel does not feel warm or hot to the touch
  • ☐ No burning smell near the panel
  • ☐ Panel brand is NOT Federal Pacific (Stab-Lok) or Zinsco — check the label inside the door if safe to open
  • ☐ All breakers are labeled (at least partially)

Section 2: Outlets Throughout the Home

  • ☐ All outlet covers are in place — no open or exposed outlets
  • ☐ No outlets feel warm or hot when you touch the cover plate
  • ☐ No discoloration or scorch marks around any outlet
  • ☐ Plugs fit snugly — outlets where plugs fall out easily need replacement
  • ☐ Bathrooms have GFCI outlets (Test/Reset buttons visible)
  • ☐ Kitchen countertop outlets within 6 feet of sink are GFCI-protected
  • ☐ Garage outlets are GFCI-protected
  • ☐ Outdoor outlets are GFCI-protected and have weatherproof covers
  • ☐ No two-prong ungrounded outlets in kitchen, bathroom, or outdoor areas
  • ☐ GFCI outlets pass the test (press Test — power cuts; press Reset — power returns)

Section 3: Wiring and Cords

  • ☐ No extension cords used as permanent wiring
  • ☐ No cords running under rugs, carpets, or through walls
  • ☐ All cords are in good condition — no fraying, cracking, or exposed copper
  • ☐ No cords pinched behind or under furniture
  • ☐ Power strips have built-in circuit breakers (a switch that trips — not just a power switch)
  • ☐ No multiple power strips chained together ("daisy-chaining")
  • ☐ Appliance cords are not tied, knotted, or bundled tightly with other cords

Section 4: Lighting

  • ☐ No bulbs exceed the maximum wattage on the fixture label
  • ☐ Recessed can lights are not covered by insulation (unless rated IC — check the label)
  • ☐ No flickering lights throughout the home (occasional on startup is normal; persistent flickering is not)
  • ☐ All light switches work correctly — no switches that control nothing
  • ☐ Bathroom lights near water are properly rated for damp locations
  • ☐ Outdoor light fixtures are rated for outdoor use and not deteriorating

Section 5: Specific Hazards to Look For

  • ☐ No electrical panel, junction boxes, or outlet boxes without covers
  • ☐ No DIY wiring visible — exposed wires, wire nuts in open spaces, or wiring not in conduit or boxes
  • ☐ Smoke detectors present within 10 feet of each sleeping area and on every floor
  • ☐ Carbon monoxide detectors present near sleeping areas (required if you have gas appliances)
  • ☐ No aluminum wiring visible (aluminum wire is silver-colored rather than copper-colored; older homes may have it in branch circuits)
  • ☐ Bathroom exhaust fans actually exhaust air — pull a tissue near the fan to check

When to Call a Licensed Electrician

Call a licensed electrician promptly if you find any of the following:

  • A warm or hot panel, outlet, or switch cover
  • Any burning smell from electrical devices or the panel
  • Scorch marks or discoloration around any outlet, switch, or the panel
  • GFCI outlets that fail the test
  • Persistent flickering lights or frequently tripping breakers
  • Federal Pacific or Zinsco panel brand
  • Any DIY wiring or open boxes
  • Outlets in bathrooms, kitchens, or outdoors without GFCI protection

Key Takeaways

  • A 30-minute walkthrough with this checklist can identify hazards before they become emergencies
  • Never open your electrical panel without qualified training — the interior remains live even with breakers off
  • GFCI outlets should be tested monthly and replaced if they fail
  • Any burning smell, warm outlet covers, or scorch marks require immediate professional attention
  • This checklist identifies items for professional evaluation — it is not a substitute for a licensed electrical inspection

Austin Generators Before Winter: Why Right Now Is the Right Time to Install

By Texas Electric and Light — Licensed Electricians, TECL #28315

Photo: Unsplash

Every February, Austin fills up with cautionary tales. The 2021 winter storm left millions of Texans without power for days — sometimes longer. Pipes froze. Food spoiled. People bundled in coats inside their own homes. For many Austin families, that week was the moment they decided: never again.

Five years later, generator installs in Central Texas are still running at record levels. If you've been thinking about it, now — before the first freeze — is exactly the right time to call. Here's why timing matters, and what to expect when you do.

Why Fall Is the Right Time (and February Is Not)

Generator demand in Austin follows a predictable pattern: when a cold front hits, the phones start ringing. By the time ice is forecast, lead times stretch from weeks to months. Units go on backorder. Permit schedules fill up. Electricians are booked out.

An install that takes two to three weeks in October can take four months in February — if you can get on the schedule at all. The families who come through a freeze with the lights on are almost always the ones who made the call before anyone was talking about weather.

The window is open now. Permit offices are moving, inventory is in stock, and our schedule has room. That changes fast once the forecast turns cold.

What a Whole-House Standby Generator Actually Does

A standby generator is permanently mounted outside your home — similar to a central AC unit — and wired directly into your electrical panel through an automatic transfer switch. When the grid goes down, the transfer switch detects the outage, the generator starts, and your house keeps running. The whole process takes about 30 seconds.

You don't flip any switches. You don't drag anything out of the garage. You don't even have to be home.

Most whole-house generators run on natural gas or propane, so there's no fuel to store. As long as your gas line is live, the generator runs — through a multi-day outage if needed.

What Size Do You Need?

This is the most common question we get. The honest answer: it depends on what you want to power.

  • Essential circuits only (lights, fridge, a few outlets, one AC unit): a 14–20 kW generator covers most homes under 2,500 sq ft.
  • Whole-house coverage (everything runs as normal): 22–26 kW for most Austin-area homes, more for larger homes or homes with electric ranges, pools, or EV chargers.

When we do an on-site estimate, we look at your panel, your square footage, and what you care most about keeping on. We'll size it right and give you options at different price points — no pressure to buy more than you need.

What the Installation Involves

A proper generator install isn't a one-afternoon job. Here's what it typically covers:

  1. On-site assessment — We evaluate your panel, available gas supply, and the best placement for the unit.
  2. Permits — Generator installs require a permit from the City of Austin or your local municipality. We handle the paperwork and inspection scheduling.
  3. Automatic transfer switch — We install the transfer switch that keeps the generator from back-feeding the grid (a safety and code requirement).
  4. Wiring and connection — The generator is wired into your panel, gas is connected, and everything is load-tested.
  5. Walk-through — We show you how the system works, what maintenance it needs, and what the annual service schedule looks like.

Most installations take one to two days once permits are in hand. Add a week or two for the permit process itself, which is why starting now matters.

What Does It Cost?

Installed costs in the Austin area typically run:

  • 14–20 kW generator: $8,000–$12,000 installed
  • 22–26 kW generator: $12,000–$18,000 installed

That range covers the unit, transfer switch, labor, and permits. Brand, gas line distance, any panel work needed, and concrete pad requirements all affect the final number — which is why a written, on-site quote is the only honest way to price it.

One thing worth knowing: some homeowner's insurance policies offer a rate reduction after a standby generator is installed, since generators can prevent freeze-related pipe damage claims. Worth a call to your insurer once it's in.

Key Takeaways

  • Generator demand spikes the moment a freeze is forecast — lead times stretch fast
  • A whole-house standby generator starts automatically in about 30 seconds, with no action needed from you
  • Most Austin homes need 14–26 kW depending on what they want to power
  • Installation takes 1–2 days of work, plus permit time — start now to be ready before February
  • Installed cost typically runs $8,000–$18,000 depending on size and site conditions

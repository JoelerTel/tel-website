Panel Upgrade Planning Guide: Everything Austin Homeowners Need to Know

By Texas Electric and Light — Licensed Electricians, TECL #28315

An electrical panel upgrade is one of the most impactful investments you can make in your Austin home's infrastructure — but it is also one of the most misunderstood. Homeowners come to us with questions about whether they really need one, how much it should cost, what exactly happens during the work, and how to avoid getting overcharged. This guide answers all of it.

When Do You Actually Need a Panel Upgrade?

Not every home needs a panel upgrade. Here are the situations where one is genuinely warranted:

Your Panel Is Physically Unsafe

Federal Pacific Electric (Stab-Lok) and Zinsco panels — very common in Austin homes built from the 1950s through the 1980s — have documented defects. FPE Stab-Lok breakers have a failure rate that means they may not trip during an overload. Zinsco breakers can fuse to the bus bar, making them impossible to turn off. If you have either of these, replacement is strongly recommended regardless of your current capacity needs.

Your Panel Is Out of Capacity

Signs your 100-amp or 150-amp panel is reaching its limits:

  • Breakers that trip regularly under normal household loads
  • You want to add an EV charger, hot tub, or additional HVAC system
  • You are planning a significant home addition or renovation
  • Lights dim noticeably when large appliances start
  • Your home inspector flagged the panel as undersized

Your Home Needs Modern Safety Features

Older panels cannot accommodate modern AFCI (arc fault) breakers, which are now required by code in bedrooms and many living areas. If you want this protection — and you should, especially in rooms where cords may be pinched or damaged — a panel upgrade that accommodates AFCI breakers is the right path.

What Size Panel Do You Need?

Most Austin homes being upgraded today go to 200-amp service, which is sufficient for the vast majority of residential applications including EV charging, modern HVAC, and smart home technology. Some situations warrant 320-amp or 400-amp service:

  • Very large homes with multiple HVAC systems
  • Homes with multiple EV chargers and significant solar systems
  • Homes with workshops or equipment requiring significant electrical capacity
  • Homes with pools, hot tubs, and extensive outdoor entertainment electrical

A licensed electrician performs a load calculation — adding up the demand of all existing and planned electrical loads — to determine the right service size for your specific home. Do not let anyone recommend a panel size without performing this calculation.

What Does a Panel Upgrade Cost in Austin?

Panel upgrade costs in Austin vary based on several factors:

  • Current panel location and service entrance — where the utility connects to your home
  • New service size — 200 amp vs. larger
  • Whether the meter base and utility connection need updating
  • Any additional circuit work included
  • Permit and inspection fees — required for all panel replacements in Austin

We do not quote specific prices in this guide because they genuinely vary by situation, and any electrician who quotes you a price without seeing your home is not giving you an accurate number. What we can tell you is that a 200-amp upgrade typically takes one day of work by a licensed electrician, requires a permit and inspection, and requires a brief utility disconnect and reconnect.

Important: Panel replacements require a permit in Austin. An electrician who offers to do the work without pulling a permit is putting you at risk — unpermitted electrical work can create insurance and resale problems and means the work was never inspected for safety.

What Happens During a Panel Upgrade?

  1. Permit pulled — your electrician submits permit application to the City of Austin or relevant jurisdiction
  2. Utility scheduled — Austin Energy disconnects power at the meter while work is done
  3. Old panel removed — existing panel pulled, meter base inspected or replaced if needed
  4. New panel installed — new panel mounted, circuits reconnected, breakers labeled
  5. Utility reconnects — power restored
  6. Inspection — a city inspector verifies the work meets code before the permit is closed

A straightforward 200-amp upgrade typically means your power is off for 4-6 hours. We schedule these to minimize impact on your day.

How to Choose the Right Electrician for a Panel Upgrade

  • Licensed — verify TECL (Texas Electrical Contractor License) number. You can check it at the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation website.
  • Pulls permits — non-negotiable. If they say a permit is not needed for a panel replacement in Austin, walk away.
  • Performs a load calculation — recommending a size without calculating your home's actual load is guesswork.
  • Provides a written quote — get the scope and price in writing before any work begins.

Key Takeaways

  • Federal Pacific and Zinsco panels should be replaced regardless of current capacity
  • 200-amp service is sufficient for most Austin homes; a load calculation determines the right size
  • Panel replacements require a permit — always, no exceptions
  • Expect your power to be off for 4-6 hours during a standard upgrade
  • Verify your electrician's TECL license before hiring

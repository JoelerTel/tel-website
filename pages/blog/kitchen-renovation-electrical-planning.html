Kitchen Renovation Electrical Planning: A Guide for Austin Homeowners

By Texas Electric and Light — Licensed Electricians, TECL #28315

Kitchen renovations are the most electrically intensive room remodels homeowners undertake. Modern kitchens have more appliances, higher circuit demands, and stricter code requirements than almost any other room in the house. Getting the electrical planning right at the start of your project — not as an afterthought — saves money, avoids delays, and ensures your new kitchen is both beautiful and safe.

Here is what Austin homeowners need to know about the electrical side of a kitchen remodel.

What the National Electrical Code Requires for Kitchens

Kitchen electrical requirements have evolved significantly over the decades. If your Austin home was built before the 1990s, your kitchen almost certainly does not meet current code — and a remodel is the time to bring it up to standard.

Current requirements for kitchen countertop circuits:

  • Two 20-amp small appliance circuits — minimum, dedicated to countertop receptacles. These cannot serve other areas of the kitchen like the dishwasher or refrigerator.
  • GFCI protection on all countertop outlets within 6 feet of a sink
  • Outlets no more than 24 inches apart along the countertop
  • Outlet at every countertop space wider than 12 inches

Dedicated Circuits Your Kitchen Likely Needs

Beyond the countertop circuits, modern kitchens have several appliances that require their own dedicated circuits. Sharing circuits with high-draw appliances causes tripped breakers and is a code violation in many cases.

  • Refrigerator — dedicated 20-amp circuit
  • Dishwasher — dedicated 20-amp circuit
  • Microwave — dedicated 20-amp circuit if built-in
  • Garbage disposal — dedicated 20-amp circuit
  • Electric range or cooktop — dedicated 50-amp 240-volt circuit
  • Wall oven — dedicated 30 or 50-amp 240-volt circuit depending on size
  • Range hood — typically on its own circuit if ventilating to exterior
  • Warming drawers, wine refrigerators, undercounter appliances — dedicated circuits recommended

Add up all of these and a major kitchen renovation may require 8-12 new circuits. This is one reason why kitchen remodels are significant electrical projects and why panel capacity matters — if your home has an older 100-amp panel, a full kitchen renovation may be the time to upgrade it to 200 amps.

Lighting: More Than Just Fixtures

Modern kitchen lighting design involves multiple lighting layers — general overhead lighting, under-cabinet task lighting, and accent or island lighting. Each of these may be on separate switched circuits, and if you want smart lighting control (dimmers, scenes), the wiring and switch location planning matters.

Under-cabinet LED lighting runs on low-voltage and may be hardwired or plug-in. Hardwired looks cleaner and is the right choice for a renovated kitchen. This requires planning the outlet or junction box locations behind upper cabinets before the cabinets are installed — not after.

Recessed lighting in kitchens needs to be rated for the installation type. Cans near or above insulation must be rated IC (insulation contact). Your electrician should verify this during rough-in.

When Do You Need a Permit for Kitchen Electrical Work?

In Austin and throughout Travis County: any electrical work beyond simple device replacement requires a permit. Adding circuits, moving outlets, adding new fixtures, and changing panel circuits all require permits and inspection.

Permits protect you. An unpermitted kitchen renovation can create problems when you sell the home, may affect your homeowner's insurance, and — most importantly — means no inspector ever verified the work was done safely.

Coordination Tip: Share your cabinet and appliance layout with your electrician before rough-in work begins. Outlet and circuit locations need to be planned around where cabinets, appliances, and the island will be. Moving an outlet after cabinets are installed is expensive. Planning it right the first time is not.

Timeline: When Does the Electrician Come In?

A kitchen remodel involves electrical work at two stages:

  1. Rough-in — after demolition and before walls/cabinets are closed up. This is when new circuits are run, boxes are set, and conduit or wiring is installed. The city inspector comes after rough-in and before anything is closed up.
  2. Trim-out — after cabinets, flooring, and drywall are complete. This is when outlets, switches, fixtures, and appliance connections are made. Final inspection follows.

Coordinate your electrician's schedule with your general contractor to ensure the rough-in inspection happens on time — delays at this stage push everything else back.

Key Takeaways

  • Modern kitchens require two dedicated 20-amp countertop circuits and GFCI protection at all countertop outlets
  • Major appliances need their own dedicated circuits — plan for 8-12 new circuits in a full remodel
  • Share your cabinet and appliance layout with your electrician before rough-in — changes after cabinets are installed are expensive
  • Electrical permits are required for kitchen remodels in Austin — always
  • A full kitchen remodel may be the right time to upgrade an older 100-amp panel

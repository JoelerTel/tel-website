Is Your Home's Electrical Ready for the Holidays? A Pre-Season Checklist for Austin Homeowners

Thanksgiving kicks off a stretch where your home runs harder than it does the rest of the year. Extra lights go up, space heaters come out of the closet, the oven runs for hours, and there may be more people — with more devices — using your circuits than usual. It's worth a quick check before the season gets busy.

Why the Holidays Put Extra Stress on Your Home's Electrical

Your home's electrical system is sized for a typical day — the usual appliances, the usual load. The holidays change that in a few ways that stack up quickly:

  • Outdoor and indoor light displays add sustained load that wasn't there before, especially on the same circuits as other devices.
  • Space heaters are one of the heaviest draws of any household appliance — many run at 1,500 watts. Plugging one into an overloaded circuit is a common cause of tripped breakers in December.
  • Holiday cooking means the oven, range, and countertop appliances (roasters, slow cookers, warming trays) running simultaneously, often in kitchens that weren't wired for that level of demand.
  • Guests bring more devices. Multiple phone chargers, laptops, portable speakers, and televisions running in guest rooms and common areas add up.
  • Older panels show their limits. A panel that was adequate for a 1980s or 1990s household is often undersized for a modern home with more devices, more lighting, and potentially an EV charger or two.

None of this means your home is in danger — most Austin homes will handle the holidays just fine. But if your panel is older, if you've had breakers trip more than once in the last year, or if you've been meaning to look at a few things, before the holiday rush is the right time to do it.

The Pre-Holiday Electrical Checklist

Work through these before Thanksgiving. Most take only a few minutes to check.

1. Walk your panel

Open the panel door and look at the breakers. Any that are sitting between ON and OFF (a tripped breaker often sits in the middle, not all the way to OFF) need to be reset and watched — repeated tripping is a sign of an overloaded or faulty circuit.
Check that every breaker slot is labeled. Unlabeled breakers make troubleshooting in the dark frustrating and slow.
Look for any signs of heat: discoloration around breakers, a burning or electrical smell, or a panel that feels warm on the outside. These are signs to call an electrician before you add more load.
If your home has a Federal Pacific Stab-Lok panel or a Zinsco/Sylvania panel, it's worth talking to an electrician. Both brands have documented failure rates and are no longer considered safe by most electrical inspectors.

2. Check your outdoor outlets and GFCI protection

Outdoor outlets must be GFCI-protected (the kind with the TEST/RESET buttons). Press TEST — the outlet should go dead. Press RESET — it should come back. If it doesn't trip or reset, the GFCI is faulty.
Make sure outdoor outlets have weatherproof covers. If you're running extension cords for lights, use covers rated for cords-in-use (they have a raised hood that lets the cord pass through while keeping rain out).
Don't run extension cords through windows or doors — it pinches the insulation and creates a fire risk. If you need an outlet in a spot that doesn't have one, that's a job for an electrician.

3. Take stock of your extension cords and power strips

Extension cords are for temporary use. Any cord that's been in place for more than a season should be replaced by a properly installed outlet.
Don't chain power strips together (plugging one into another). It bypasses the overload protection in both strips.
Throw out any extension cord or power strip with cracked insulation, discoloration, a plug that gets warm, or a loose connection.
Use outdoor-rated cords for anything outside. Standard indoor cords aren't weatherproof.

4. Plan your space heater placement

Plug space heaters directly into a wall outlet — not into a power strip or extension cord. They draw too much current for most strips and cords to handle safely.
Don't run a space heater on the same circuit as other heavy appliances. If your breaker trips when you turn on a heater, that circuit is at capacity.
Keep space heaters at least 3 feet from curtains, furniture, and bedding.

5. Test your smoke detectors

Press the test button on every smoke detector. If you haven't replaced the batteries in the last year, do it now — before the cooking and candles start.
Smoke detectors older than 10 years should be replaced. The sensing element degrades over time.

💡 Austin code tip

The City of Austin requires interconnected smoke alarms on every level of the home and in every sleeping area. If yours aren't interconnected (when one goes off, they all go off), that's worth fixing before you have guests staying over.

Signs Your Panel May Not Be Ready for the Extra Load

Most breaker trips during the holidays aren't dangerous — they're the system doing what it's supposed to do. But some patterns are worth looking at before you add more load:

  • Breakers that trip repeatedly on the same circuit, especially when nothing is obviously overloaded, can indicate a wiring problem or a failing breaker.
  • Lights that dim when you turn on an appliance (especially on a different circuit) can point to a panel issue, loose connections, or inadequate service coming in from the utility.
  • A panel that's nearly full — if every slot has a breaker in it and you're adding a new circuit or EV charger, you need a panel upgrade or a subpanel before anything else can be added safely.
  • An older fuse box (the round glass fuses rather than breakers that flip) is a sign the home's electrical was sized for a much older load profile. These can be unsafe and are typically flagged during home sales and insurance renewals.

⚠️ When to call an electrician before the holidays

If you notice any of the following, it's worth having someone look at it now rather than on December 23rd: burning or electrical smell near outlets or the panel, outlets or switches that are warm to the touch, flickering lights on circuits that aren't shared with heavy appliances, or any breaker that won't reset and hold. These aren't panic situations — they're just better handled before the season gets busy.

What About Holiday Lighting Load?

LED holiday lights have changed this calculation significantly. A string of incandescent C7 bulbs can draw 5–7 watts per bulb. A comparable LED string might draw 0.5 watts per bulb — about one-tenth the load. If you've switched to LEDs, you've probably already noticed that the same outlet and circuit can handle much more than it used to.

That said, if you're planning an elaborate outdoor display with multiple heavy-draw elements (large inflatable decorations, projectors, heating elements in outdoor props), it's worth calculating the total wattage before you plug everything in. Add up the wattage of everything on a single circuit and compare it to the circuit's rating (usually 15 or 20 amps — 1,800 or 2,400 watts). Stay below 80% of that rating for continuous loads.

Quick load calculation

Volts × Amps = Watts. A 20-amp, 120V circuit can handle up to 2,400 watts, but you should stay at or below 1,920 watts (80%) for anything running for more than a few minutes. Add up the wattage ratings on all devices sharing a circuit before plugging in that extra light display or heater.

Austin-Specific Things to Keep in Mind

Central Texas winters are mild compared to most of the country, but we do get cold snaps — sometimes sharp ones — that send everyone to their space heaters at the same time. Austin Energy's grid has gotten more attention since the 2021 winter storm, but your home's internal wiring is a separate issue from the grid. Even with stable power coming in, an older panel or overloaded circuits in your own home can cause problems during a cold snap when demand is highest.

It's also worth noting that holiday cooking in Austin often includes extended outdoor grilling and smoker use alongside indoor oven cooking. If you're running an electric smoker or outdoor cooking appliance from a standard outdoor outlet, check that the outlet is rated for the load and that the GFCI is in good shape.

The Short Version

You don't need to do anything dramatic before the holidays. For most Austin homes, a quick walk through the checklist above takes 20 minutes and gives you peace of mind. If something looks off — a breaker that keeps tripping, an outlet that's warm, a panel that's full — that's worth a call to an electrician while schedules are still open. Things get busier once December arrives.

We provide free on-site estimates for electrical work. For a general inspection where you want us to look at the panel and walk through concerns, call us and we can discuss what makes sense for your situation.

My breaker tripped once during Thanksgiving last year. Should I be worried?

One trip isn't necessarily a cause for concern — it may just mean that circuit hit its limit. If it's happening repeatedly on the same circuit, or if the breaker won't hold after you reset it, that's worth looking at. Same if the circuit trips even when the load seems light.

Can I add a new outlet for holiday lights?

Yes. Adding an outdoor outlet (or moving an existing one to a more useful location) is a common job. It requires a permit in the City of Austin, which we handle. We can tell you what's involved after seeing the location.

Is it safe to run a space heater on a 15-amp circuit?

A standard 1,500-watt space heater draws about 12.5 amps — close to the limit for a 15-amp circuit on its own. If anything else is on that circuit (a lamp, a phone charger), you're right at or over capacity. Best practice is to plug the heater directly into a 20-amp wall outlet and nothing else on that circuit.

When should I schedule electrical work before the holidays?

The earlier the better. November is when schedules start to fill up. If you're thinking about a panel inspection, an outlet addition, or anything else before December, give us a call now and we can usually get out to you within the week.

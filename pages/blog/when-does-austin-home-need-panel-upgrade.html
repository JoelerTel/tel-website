When Does an Austin Home Need a Panel Upgrade? A Licensed Electrician Explains

By Texas Electric and Light — Licensed Electricians, TECL #28315

Your electrical panel is the heart of your home's electrical system. Every circuit in your house — lights, outlets, appliances, AC, EV charger — runs through it. Most Austin homeowners never think about their panel until something goes wrong. By then, you're dealing with tripped breakers, flickering lights, or worse, a safety hazard that's been building behind your walls for years.

We upgrade electrical panels across Austin, Dripping Springs, Buda, Kyle, and the surrounding Hill Country every week. Here's what we tell every homeowner who asks us whether they need one.

Quick Answer: You Likely Need a Panel Upgrade If...

  • Your home has a 60-amp or 100-amp panel and you're adding an EV charger, hot tub, or major appliance
  • Breakers trip regularly even when you're not running anything unusual
  • Your panel is more than 25-30 years old
  • You have a Federal Pacific, Zinsco, or Pushmatic panel
  • Your home insurance company is requiring an upgrade
  • You're doing a major renovation or addition

Why Austin Homes Need Panel Upgrades More Than Ever

Austin's housing stock is aging — a large number of homes in neighborhoods like Hyde Park, Travis Heights, Bouldin Creek, and the older parts of Round Rock and Cedar Park were built in the 1960s through 1980s with electrical systems sized for the appliances of that era. A 100-amp panel made sense when a home had a refrigerator, a window AC unit, a washer, and a few lights. It doesn't make sense when that same home now has central AC, a dishwasher, two refrigerators, a home office with multiple monitors, smart home devices, and a Tesla in the garage.

At the same time, Austin's newer homes in areas like Dripping Springs, Steiner Ranch, and Westlake are being built with 200-amp panels as standard — but even those can get stretched as EV charging, battery backup systems, and all-electric kitchens become the norm.

Signs Your Panel Is Struggling

1. Breakers That Trip Repeatedly

A breaker that trips once in a while is doing its job — protecting your home from an overloaded circuit. A breaker that trips regularly, or one you've gotten used to resetting every week, is telling you something. Either that circuit is consistently overloaded (meaning you need more circuits), or the breaker itself is worn out and needs replacement. Either way, it needs attention.

Never do this: If a breaker keeps tripping and you've replaced it with a higher-amperage breaker to stop the tripping — that's a serious fire hazard. The breaker is rated to match the wire it protects. A 20-amp breaker on 15-amp wire means the wire can overheat and start a fire before the breaker trips. We see this more than you'd think.

2. You Have a 60-Amp or 100-Amp Panel

60-amp panels are obsolete for modern homes. Full stop. If your home still has one, you're almost certainly undersized and your home insurance may not renew without an upgrade. 100-amp panels are common in homes built before 1990 and can work fine for smaller homes with modest electrical loads — but the moment you add an EV charger (which needs 50 amps on its own), you're likely at your limit.

3. Your Panel Is a Known Problem Brand

Three panel brands have well-documented safety issues and should be replaced regardless of age:

  • Federal Pacific Electric (FPE) / Stab-Lok — breakers have been documented to fail to trip during overloads, creating fire risk. Common in Austin homes built between 1950 and 1990.
  • Zinsco / GTE-Sylvania — similar failure mode, breakers can weld shut and fail to trip. Also common in older Austin homes.
  • Pushmatic — breakers wear out and become unreliable over time. Replacement parts are no longer manufactured.

If you're not sure what brand your panel is, open the cover and look at the breakers. FPE panels often say "Stab-Lok" on the breakers. Zinsco breakers are often colorful (red, blue, green). Pushmatic breakers push straight in rather than flipping.

4. You're Adding High-Demand Equipment

The most common reason we upgrade panels in Austin right now is EV chargers. A Level 2 EV charger requires a dedicated 240V, 50-amp circuit. If your panel is already near capacity, that circuit simply won't fit without an upgrade. The same applies to:

  • Whole-home generators or battery backup systems (Tesla Powerwall, Generac PWRcell)
  • Hot tubs and pool equipment
  • Workshop subpanels
  • All-electric kitchen renovations (induction range, double oven)
  • Home additions or ADUs

5. Your Insurance Company Is Asking for It

This is increasingly common in Central Texas. Home insurance underwriters are scrutinizing electrical systems more carefully, especially in older homes. If you've received a notice that your policy won't renew unless certain electrical upgrades are made — take it seriously. We can often work quickly to get the upgrade done before your deadline.

What a Panel Upgrade Actually Involves

A lot of homeowners put off panel upgrades because they picture major construction — walls torn open, days without power. The reality is usually much less disruptive than that.

A typical panel upgrade in Austin looks like this:

  1. Pull a permit — required by the City of Austin and all surrounding municipalities. This protects you and ensures the work is inspected.
  2. Coordinate with Austin Energy or Pedernales Electric — the utility has to temporarily disconnect power at the meter while we swap the panel. This is usually scheduled a few days out.
  3. Replace the panel — the old panel comes out, the new one goes in. All existing circuits are reconnected. For a standard upgrade, this takes most of a single day.
  4. Inspection — a city inspector verifies the work meets code before power is restored. We schedule this and handle it.
  5. Power restored — typically the same day or the morning after.

Most Austin homeowners are without power for 4-6 hours during the actual swap. For the rest of the project timeline, your power is on.

What Does a Panel Upgrade Cost in Austin?

We get this question constantly, and the honest answer is: it depends on your specific situation. Here are the main factors:

  • Current panel size and location — upgrading from 100A to 200A is straightforward. Moving the panel location or going to 400A adds cost.
  • Service entrance condition — if your weatherhead, meter base, or service entrance cable needs replacement (common in older homes), that's additional work.
  • Permit and inspection fees — City of Austin permit fees vary by project scope.
  • Brand and quality of the new panel — we install Eaton, Square D, and Siemens panels depending on the application.

For most Austin homes, a standard 200-amp panel upgrade runs between $2,500 and $4,500 fully installed, permitted, and inspected. If additional work is needed — new meter base, service entrance, or sub-panel — costs go up from there. We give detailed written quotes before any work begins, no surprises.

Tip for Dripping Springs and Hays County homeowners: If you're served by Pedernales Electric Cooperative (PEC) rather than Austin Energy, the coordination process is slightly different and can take a bit longer to schedule. We work with PEC regularly and know the process well.

How Long Does a Panel Last?

A quality panel installed correctly should last 25-40 years. The breakers inside can wear out sooner, especially if they've been tripping frequently. If your panel is approaching or past 30 years old, it's worth having a licensed electrician look at it — even if you haven't had obvious problems. Age-related wear, loose connections, and corrosion can create hazards that aren't visible from the outside.

Do I Need a Panel Upgrade or Just More Circuits?

Not every electrical capacity problem requires a full panel replacement. Sometimes the issue is that your existing circuits are overloaded and you need additional circuits added — which can be done without replacing the panel if there's available space and amperage. During a site visit, we'll assess your current panel, calculate your actual load, and tell you honestly whether a full upgrade is necessary or whether adding circuits will solve the problem. We're not going to sell you a panel upgrade you don't need.

Not Sure If Your Panel Needs an Upgrade?

We offer panel assessments for Austin area homeowners. We'll look at your current panel, assess your load, and give you a straight answer — and a written quote if work is needed. No pressure, no upsell.

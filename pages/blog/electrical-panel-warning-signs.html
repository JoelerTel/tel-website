5 Warning Signs Your Electrical Panel Needs Immediate Attention

By Texas Electric and Light — Licensed Electricians, TECL #28315

Your electrical panel is the heart of your home's electrical system. It distributes power to every circuit in your house and — when working correctly — protects you from dangerous overloads and faults. The problem is that panels are out of sight, usually in a garage or utility closet, and most homeowners never look at them until something goes wrong.

By the time a panel fails dramatically, there have almost always been warning signs for months or years. As licensed Austin electricians who inspect and replace panels regularly, we want you to know what those warning signs look like — so you can catch problems before they become emergencies.

Warning Sign 1: Breakers That Trip Frequently

A breaker that trips once in a while after you plug in something high-draw — a vacuum, a space heater — is doing its job. A breaker that trips regularly under normal household use, or one that trips and then trips again immediately after you reset it, is telling you something is wrong.

The most common causes of frequent tripping are overloaded circuits (too many devices drawing too much power on one circuit), a failing breaker that can no longer hold its rated load, or a short circuit in the wiring on that branch. None of these resolve themselves. A breaker that won't stay reset is a fire risk and needs professional evaluation immediately.

Never tape a breaker in the "on" position or replace a breaker with a higher-rated one to stop it from tripping. This bypasses the only protection between faulty wiring and a fire.

Warning Sign 2: A Panel That Feels Warm or Hot

Electrical panels generate some heat — that is normal. A panel that feels noticeably warm to the touch, or hot, is not. Heat indicates resistance somewhere in the system — a loose connection, a failing breaker, or wiring that is undersized for the load it is carrying.

Loose connections are particularly dangerous because they arc — creating small electrical sparks inside your panel. Arcing generates intense heat and is one of the leading causes of electrical fires. If your panel feels hot, do not wait to call a licensed electrician.

Warning Sign 3: Burning Smell or Scorch Marks

A burning smell from your electrical panel is an emergency. Turn off your main breaker and call a licensed electrician — and if you smell smoke or see any sign of active burning, leave the house and call 911 first.

Scorch marks on or around the panel, on outlet covers, or on breakers indicate that arcing has already occurred. Even if the burning has stopped, the underlying cause has not been fixed. This requires immediate professional inspection.

Warning Sign 4: Lights That Flicker or Dim When Appliances Run

When your AC kicks on and your lights dim noticeably, that is a sign your panel is struggling with the load. Large motors draw a surge of current when starting up, and if your panel and wiring can't handle it cleanly, you see it in your lights.

Occasional minor dimming when a large motor starts is common in older homes. Significant, persistent dimming — or flickering in multiple rooms — points to a panel at or near capacity, a loose main connection, or a problem with your utility service entrance. All of these need professional evaluation.

Warning Sign 5: Your Panel Is a Known Problem Brand

Certain electrical panels manufactured between the 1950s and 1980s have been found to have design defects that create fire risks even when functioning normally. The two most common in Austin homes:

  • Federal Pacific Electric (FPE) Stab-Lok panels — Very common in Austin homes built from the 1950s through the 1980s. The Stab-Lok breakers have a documented failure rate — they sometimes fail to trip during an overload, removing the only protection against fires.
  • Zinsco panels — Also common in older Central Texas homes. Zinsco breakers can fuse to the bus bar, making them impossible to turn off and removing all overload protection.

If you don't know your panel brand, open the panel door and look for the manufacturer name. If you see "Federal Pacific," "Stab-Lok," or "Zinsco," call a licensed electrician for an evaluation. Insurance companies are increasingly refusing to insure homes with these panels, and for good reason.

Key Takeaways

  • Breakers that trip repeatedly under normal use need professional evaluation — not repeated resetting
  • A warm or hot panel indicates dangerous heat buildup that requires immediate attention
  • Any burning smell from an electrical panel is an emergency — turn off the main breaker and call immediately
  • Significant light flickering when appliances run points to a panel approaching capacity
  • Federal Pacific and Zinsco panels should be evaluated and considered for replacement regardless of visible symptoms
